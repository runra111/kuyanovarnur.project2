02 — The product

Meet ENGRAM.

A lightweight, non-invasive headset that records an expert’s experience — and lets a student relive it.

  • 1
    Neural readerVisor sensors read attention and focus.
  • 2
    Memory coreRecords, stores and plays back a lesson.
  • 3
    Side modulesSound and touch cues at the temples.

Drag to rotate · click a point

03 — The problem

We forget what
we only hear.

  • PassiveLectures and textbooks transfer facts, not experience.
  • Risky & costlyReal practice needs labs, patients, aircraft — and one mistake is expensive.
  • UnequalThe best teacher is in one room, for thirty students.

OECD’s PISA 2022 flags falling student engagement as a common factor behind declining results.

04 — Target audience

Who is it for?

A

Students

Ages 13–22. School and university learners who need to understand a skill, not just memorise it.

B

Vocational & medical trainees

Surgery, chemistry, welding, flying: dangerous or costly to practise — safe to relive.

C

Teachers & schools

Experts become memory authors: record a lesson once, teach thousands of students.

05 — How it works

Record. Edit. Relive.

01

Record

An expert wears ENGRAM during the real thing: a surgery, an experiment, a flight.

02

Edit

The lesson gets layers: expert thoughts, gaze, body signals, warnings and quizzes.

03

Relive

A student sees it first-person. Pause, rewind, look around, switch layers: as often as needed.

09 — Responsible by design

Built on trust.

Read-onlyENGRAM never writes to the brain. It only reads signals and plays media.
Consent firstOnly experts who agree can record. Students never record themselves.
PrivateData is encrypted on the device and stays inside the school.
Age-safeSession limits and teacher-approved content for every grade.
